What I do

Four areas, and the evidence under each.

Behavioural science is the training underneath all of it. It shows up as a constraint on the design rather than a coat of paint on the end — it is why I ask what carried an effect rather than only whether there was one, and why I would rather measure a change in behaviour than a satisfaction score.

01 — AI engineering

Building language‑model systems, and knowing when they are lying.

Generating fluent text is the easy half. Proving it is faithful to the evidence is the half that decides whether anyone can use it.

The evaluation gate

Sole designer and implementer of the pipeline every AI persona passes before it reaches a client. It searches a vector store for the ground‑truth evidence behind each claim, puts the pair to an ensemble of five judges, and uses agreement between them as a confidence score. Where the judges disagree, the claim goes to a person rather than being averaged away.

Validation cut from 0.5–1 analyst‑days to 15 minutes per iteration

Knowing the number means something

A held‑out benchmark scoring persona fidelity against real answers the system had never seen, with the match criterion defined separately for each data type, and the judges themselves calibrated against a 50‑pair reference set before being trusted.

88–90% alignment on held‑out data

Multi‑granularity retrieval — built and tested, not deployed

A prototype, not a shipped feature. A retrieval router that chooses how coarsely to chunk a document for each individual query instead of fixing one chunk size for everything: hierarchical chunking, labels generated by cosine similarity rather than by hand, and a small PyTorch network trained to predict the right granularity. Complete and working, but not integrated into the live product and not benchmarked against the fixed‑chunk system it would replace.

~56% top‑1 and ~72% top‑2 granularity accuracy in testing

Making the data embeddable in the first place

Around 17,000 question–answer pairs generated from interview transcripts to train that router, and a transformation that turns tabular client data into natural language a model can read — including the codebook that tells it what each variable means.

Agents measured on behaviour, not satisfaction

Co‑built and evaluated three conversational agents with around 300 real users, measuring pre/post change in intention rather than whether people enjoyed the conversation.

02 — Statistics and experiments

Designing studies that can actually answer the question.

An experiment is only worth running if its result would change what you do. Most of my statistical work is in that design step, and in the translation back out to the people who have to act on it.

NHS lung health check invitations

First author on a five‑arm randomised experiment testing how the wording of an invitation letter affects whether people attend an NHS Targeted Lung Health Check. I ran the full analytical pipeline and translated the findings for clinical and policy stakeholders.

n = 3,274 · under review at Nature Communications · preprint on medRxiv

Which patients respond to which doctors

A randomised experiment for a global pharmaceutical client testing three communication styles across four patient types in two markets. One of two analysts; I led the counterfactual mediation analysis — whether health literacy, emotion and trust in the doctor carry the effect — and presented the headline results to the client.

~6,000 analysed participants. The findings are the client’s and are not disclosed here.

The methods underneath

Logistic and covariate‑adjusted regression, counterfactual mediation, mixed‑effects models, bootstrapping, difference‑in‑differences and switchback designs, structural equation modelling, and exploratory and confirmatory factor analysis.

03 — Machine learning

When your data was never designed to be joined up.

Real datasets rarely arrive in one shape. A lot of the difficulty is upstream of the model: deciding what can legitimately be compared before anything is clustered or predicted.

The segmentation engine behind the personas

Built the unsupervised pipeline that turns raw respondent data into the segments a product is built around. Respondents come from different sources answering similar but not identical questionnaires, so it reduces across sources with PCA first, derives segments with UMAP and HDBSCAN, and uses random forest for feature importance within and across datasets.

Validating a model somebody else built

Led the analysis validating and extending a client’s patient segmentation model for a population it had not been built on — linear discriminant analysis, confusion matrices, permutation tests.

The client adopted the revised tool

Prediction, compared honestly

Research lead on an AI‑powered psychometric prediction tool, comparing XGBoost, random forest, elastic net, a PyTorch neural network and plain regression on large psychometric datasets, rather than assuming the most complex model wins. Variable‑importance methods then steered the product toward a cheaper instrument.

Reinforcement learning — built, not shipped

A recommender using a multi‑armed bandit with Thompson sampling to personalise behavioural interventions for a digital‑health platform, alongside causal discovery in R to learn Bayesian graphs from timestamped activity data. The engagement ended before it was deployed.

04 — Research and advisory

Explaining a model to the people who have to act on it.

A model nobody trusts does not get used, and work only one person can run is not an asset. Peer review is where the standard of evidence gets set; the advisory work is where it has to survive contact with a client.

Peer‑reviewed research

First author on the NHS lung health check trial, under review at Nature Communications. Second author with N. Harvey on Ethical compromise, valuing life, and the Fechner’s Law, in preparation for Cognition, where I led the regression, mediation analysis and visualisation. Honorary Research Fellow at UCL, Department of Experimental Psychology, since December 2023.

SPUDM 2025, scheduled speaker · UCL Centre for Behaviour Change Healthcare Conference 2025, Lisbon

Architecture advice, and making the work reusable

Advised a client’s leadership on the end‑to‑end architecture of an AI‑agent system — data flows, the SQL and vector‑database backend, retrieval, and how several agents would collaborate — with a delivery roadmap and training workshops. Productised repeated manual work into documented, version‑controlled pipelines the team can run without me.

An AI‑adoption diagnostic — at design stage

Leading the design of a diagnostic that segments employees by where they are on the AI adoption journey and which barriers are holding them back, so an organisation can target interventions instead of running the same training for everybody. Construct selection, a literature review of what predicts acceptance and use of generative AI, and a validation plan built on factor analysis and structural equation modelling.

At design stage. No results yet.

Reporting that runs itself

Built a scheduled Microsoft Fabric pipeline — Python steps and Power Query over MySQL — refreshing eight times a day behind client‑facing Power BI reporting, and co‑presented it with the CEO. Introduced version‑controlled collaboration and wrote the deployment and reproducibility documentation.
